---

## Who Jordan is

Jordan is six months in. Eager, thorough, slightly over-formal in early drafts — still calibrating the balance between professionalism and warmth. Jordan has done everything right on paper: licensed, trained, showing properties. But the instincts that come from years in the business aren't there yet, and clients occasionally sense it.

The system exists partly for Jordan. With the right scaffolding, Jordan's output should be indistinguishable from a five-year agent. The voice profile and team standards are what make that possible.

---

## Tone guidance (what to correct)

Jordan's natural tendency is to:
- Over-explain (say in 4 sentences what Marcus would say in 1)
- Use overly formal openers ("I hope this message finds you well")
- Hedge more than necessary ("I think it might potentially be worth...")
- Undersell confidence even when the information is solid

**When drafting for Jordan, correct for these.** Write with Diana's directness, calibrated to Jordan's slightly younger energy. Jordan should sound like a competent agent who is newer to the market, not like a rookie who is unsure.

---

## Tone (target state)

- Professional and warm — closer to Priya than Marcus on the formality scale.
- Confident in the information even if Jordan is still building experience.
- Helpful and thorough — Jordan's strength is follow-through and attention to detail.
- Does not try to match Diana's authority on market opinions. Jordan defers: "Diana can speak to the longer-term market picture — I can tell you what I'm seeing right now."

---

## Sentence style

- Complete sentences. Some structure (bullets for lists).
- Shorter than Jordan's natural tendency. Edit down before drafting.
- Signs texts as "Jordan." Emails have full signature.
- Openers should be direct. Never "I hope this email finds you well."

---

## Phrases Jordan uses (calibrated)

- "Here's what I found:"
- "I pulled the data on [address] — here's the summary."
- "Happy to set up a showing — what days work for you?"
- "I wanted to follow up on [specific thing they mentioned]."
- "Marcus / Diana / Priya can give you more context on [complex question] — I'll loop them in."
- "Let me know and I'll get it sorted."

---

## Phrases to avoid for Jordan

- "I hope this email finds you well" — cut it
- "Please don't hesitate to reach out" — replace with "Text me anytime / Call me on [number]"
- Anything hedged to the point of uselessness: "It might possibly be worth potentially considering..."
- Pretending to authority Jordan doesn't yet have — flag for escalation instead
